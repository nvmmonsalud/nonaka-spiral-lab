# Knowledge Spiral Lab: Japanese pilots and a path to scale

Research memo · 2026-10-29 · Based on a static inspection of this checkout and primary sources accessed on that date. Recommendations, pilot targets and business assumptions below are proposals, not validated results. No customer interviews, production deployment, model calls or security testing were performed.

## Decision in brief

Position the current product as a **facilitated learning and knowledge-transfer design tool**, then test an expert-reviewed knowledge workflow with one Japanese manufacturing team. Retain a free, synthetic simulator as the workshop entry point. Do not sell its score as a measure or prediction of business performance. The first product hypothesis is that a guided interview, evidence-linked draft and hands-on practice can reduce the effort to produce a trustworthy work instruction and improve a novice's ability to complete a bounded task; neither effect is established for this project.

Japan's 2026 manufacturing white paper discusses skills transfer and the continued employment of older workers as a means of passing on skills. The SME Agency's 2025 white paper describes structural labor shortages; an official case describes a manufacturer researching AI-assisted skills transfer with a university. These sources support the relevance of the problem, not demand for this product or the effectiveness of its proposed workflow. [METI manufacturing white paper 2026, overview](https://www.meti.go.jp/report/whitepaper/mono/2026/pdf/gaiyo.pdf); [SME Agency, 2025 overview](https://www.chusho.meti.go.jp/pamflet/hakusyo/2025/chusho/gaiyo.html); [SME Agency case](https://www.chusho.meti.go.jp/pamflet/hakusyo/2025/chusho/b1_1_4.html).

## What exists and what the evidence means

- **Observed in repository:** `web/src/components/SpiralSimulator.tsx` is a React client component with a canvas, sliders, preset loading, random particle movement and conversion rules. `web/data/presets.json` contains scenario parameters. `web/src/app/{page,simulator/page,research/page}.tsx` provide the app pages. `web/package.json` lists Next.js 16.3.0 and React 19.2.8. The inspected app has no database, model client, authentication or server API; README describes it as client-side. This is a static code assessment, not a live deployment audit.
- **Simulated:** `Math.random()` controls conversion, scripted hallucination, context failure and transfer. A transfer can increment the cycle count without traversing all SECI stages; the score increases by `level * 10` on completed cycles or transfers and is not a validated maturity or ROI metric. Time, particle count, code constants and animation influence it. The current implementation is not seeded or reproducible. These events do not establish actual machine cognition, a measured hallucination rate, an organization's capability, or cause and effect.
- **Prior project research:** `results/paper.md`, `results/case-studies.md`, `results/secai-ai-matrix.md` and `research/sources.md` frame SECI, machine knowledge and cases. `research/expansion-research.md` proposes a maturity assessor, but no assessor route was found in the inspected `web/src/app`. Treat paper interpretations and vendor-reported claims such as the cited 5× accuracy improvement as hypotheses or attributed claims, not independently replicated project outcomes. Its historical assertion of uniqueness is unverified. A score questionnaire would also need validation before being called a maturity assessment.
- **Real AI, proposed only:** a model-enabled interview or draft generator would send selected content to an actual provider, create probabilistic outputs and incur cost and data-processing obligations. Clearly label output as AI-generated and unverified until a named expert approves it. A simulation preset named after a real organization is an illustrative interpretation, not evidence that the organization uses this product.

## Japanese business use cases and pitch

Start with one buyer, one workflow and a measurable output; the other use cases are hypotheses for discovery, not proof of demand.

| Priority and buyer | Workflow and SECI interpretation | Proposed artifact and human decision | Why the buyer might care / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Manufacturing plant manager and training/quality lead | Expert and novice discuss a non-safety-critical setup or defect-triage example (socialization); expert records conditions, exceptions and observations (externalization); reviewer connects existing SOPs (combination); novice practices under supervision (internalization). | Versioned, cited work-instruction draft, conditions and escalation checklist; expert and quality owner approve before use. | Skills succession and reduced reliance on a few experts are plausible needs. Exclude safety-critical settings, equipment control and regulated quality decisions from initial pilot. |
| 2. B2B service or field-maintenance manager | Capture a resolved incident and what changed the diagnosis, link to approved manuals, then run a novice tabletop exercise. | Reviewed troubleshooting playbook with source links, date, applicability and escalation trigger. | Hypothesis: faster onboarding and fewer repeated questions. Risks include obsolete manuals and customer identifiers; use sanitized material. |
| 3. SME owner / succession planning or sales operations lead | Capture handover decisions and customer-facing exceptions, have a second person practice a mock handover. | Approved handover checklist and ownership map, with sensitive client details omitted. | Hypothesis: continuity when staff change. Trade secrets and relationship context make authorization and source ownership central. |

Suggested Japanese opening for a discovery meeting:

> ベテランの判断を、現場で確認できる形に残し、次の担当者が練習できるようにする実証をご一緒しませんか。まずは機密情報を使わず、一つの作業を対象に、作成・確認の時間と習得状況を測ります。AIが作った内容は、担当者の確認後にのみ採用します。

English: “Would you run a small pilot with us to document an expert's judgment in a form the team can verify and practice? We would start with one task and no confidential information, measuring preparation, review and learning. People approve any AI-generated content.” This is a proposal, not a claim of proven savings. The likely economic buyer is a plant or operations leader; quality, IT/security, HR/training and worker representatives should agree on scope and usage before access. IPA's 2025 survey reports over 80% of surveyed Japanese companies already undertaking DX reported a shortage of people to drive it; the survey population and question are narrower than all companies and do not establish product demand. [IPA DX Trends 2025](https://www.ipa.go.jp/pressrelease/2025/press20250626.html).

**Discovery questions:** Which one task is frequently escalated? What happens when the expert is absent? Which records can be shared? Who owns sign-off and updates? What is the existing onboarding baseline? What procurement, data location and union/employee consultation requirements apply? Would the sponsor continue if review takes as long as writing directly?

## Product sequence and acceptance gates

| Priority | Deliverable | Acceptance evidence / dependency |
|---|---|---|
| P0: credible workshop | Japanese-language explanation and accessible labels, explicit “illustrative simulation” and score caveat, source notes, pause/reset; separate a seeded, versioned deterministic simulation engine from canvas and capture parameters, seed, model version and duration. Keep data local by default. | Same version, seed and inputs reproduce a fixed-step event sequence; labels tested with Japanese speakers; no suggestion that score is actual productivity. Verify animation performance with capped particles and throttled UI updates. |
| P1: manual pilot | Facilitator interview template, source/permission register, draft → expert review → approved/retired lifecycle, export and supervised practice checklist; synthetic or customer-approved sanitized examples. No AI required. | Each approved instruction has an owner, evidence, applicability, revision date and reviewer; rejected claims retained as review records where permitted. Measure the baseline and review burden. |
| P2: private workspace | Authenticated tenant, role-based access, version history, audit, retention/deletion, backups and secure ingestion; start with one customer and limited users. | Isolation and authorization tests, restoration/deletion drills, documented incident response and customer-approved processing agreement before real customer material. |
| P3: optional real AI | Opt-in provider adapter for interview prompts and source-grounded drafts; citations to permitted passages, abstention, expert approval, evaluation set, per-tenant budget and kill switch. | Evidence-linked output and access filtering tested; quality, safety, latency and total human review time compared with manual baseline. No autonomous changes to equipment or systems. |
| P4: expansion | Add integrations and multi-site workflows only after pilot evidence; SSO, permission-aware sync, quotas, monitoring and tenant-specific policies. | Explicit business case, security review, source ACL deletion propagation and cost/operational targets; no cross-tenant learning by default. |

The P0/P1 work can be useful without a backend or a paid model. A questionnaire may be used as a structured conversation aid, with transparent non-validated scoring and no worker ranking; validate reliability, interpretation and fairness before claiming diagnostic maturity.

## Proposed architecture, scaling and boundaries

**Recommendation:** keep the existing Next.js app and extract the simulation into a pure TypeScript engine. For a private pilot, add a Next.js server-side backend for frontend, a managed PostgreSQL database, private object storage and an asynchronous worker only when needed. This is an architectural option, not an implemented or provider-selected stack. Keep the public synthetic simulation separate from private customer workspaces and their credentials.

```text
Browser: synthetic simulator / private workspace
  -> authenticated Next.js route handlers or server actions
  -> authorization + tenant-aware data access layer
  -> PostgreSQL (records, policy, versions, audit) + private object storage (approved files)
  -> queue/outbox -> worker (parse, index, optional model adapter)
  -> permission-filtered retrieval -> optional AI provider -> draft -> expert approval
```

Suggested core tables: `tenants`, `users`, `memberships` (role and status), `projects`, `sources` and immutable `source_versions` (provenance, permission/classification, checksum and deletion state), `knowledge_items` and `item_versions` (draft/approved/retired, owner and applicability), `citations` (version and passage), `reviews`, `practice_attempts`, `simulation_runs` (seed/engine version/config, labeled synthetic), `audit_events` and `jobs`. Put `tenant_id` on tenant-owned rows, use tenant-scoped composite foreign keys and unique constraints to prevent cross-tenant links, and define indexes for tenant/status/date. Store only the minimum raw content necessary. An immutable revision history still needs a defined deletion/anonymization process for personal data and backups.

**Example contract:** `POST /api/projects` creates a scoped project; `POST /api/sources` initiates a size-limited upload and creates a pending source; `GET /api/knowledge-items/:id` returns only authorized versions; `POST /api/knowledge-items/:id/reviews` records an expert decision; `POST /api/drafts` queues an optional generation job and returns a job ID; `GET /api/jobs/:id` returns tenant-scoped status. Every request validates schema, session, membership, resource ownership and role on the server; mutations use idempotency keys when retried, controlled limits, predictable errors and audit metadata. Use short-lived private download URLs only after authorization. Do not expose model keys to the browser. Next.js explicitly advises treating server actions and route handlers as public-facing endpoints and checking authorization; its guidance recommends a centralized data-access layer. [Next.js authentication](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/authentication); [Next.js mutating data](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/getting-started/mutating-data).

**Isolation and scale:** PostgreSQL row-level security can provide defense in depth, with default-deny policies, tenant context bound safely to each transaction, and a non-owner application role without `BYPASSRLS`. Owners and privileged roles can bypass policies, so enforce least-privilege GRANTs, secure worker access and cross-tenant negative tests; RLS is not a replacement for application authorization. [PostgreSQL row security](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-rowsecurity.html). At pilot scale use one managed database and simple full-text search. Add per-tenant quotas, indexed pagination, connection pooling, job concurrency limits, retries with deduplication, dead-letter handling and observability as load warrants. Only add vector search after a tested need: retain tenant and source ACLs at ingestion **and retrieval**, propagate permission changes/deletions to indexes and caches, and prevent shared caches from serving private data to another tenant. For data residency or contractual isolation, evaluate dedicated databases or customer-controlled deployment as an explicit higher-cost option.

**Model boundary:** retrieve only authorized, currently applicable passages and return citation identifiers; provide “insufficient evidence” rather than forcing an answer. Treat retrieved documents as untrusted instructions, never allow them to alter system permissions or trigger actions, and require expert approval before publication. A citation indicates provenance, not truth. OWASP identifies prompt injection and sensitive-information disclosure as LLM application risks; retrieval alone does not eliminate them. [OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications 2025](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/assets/PDF/OWASP-Top-10-for-LLMs-v2025.pdf).

## Privacy, governance and security before customer data

1. Agree purpose, data categories, roles and instructions in writing. Default to fictional or sanitized examples; do not assume pseudonymization makes a record anonymous. Avoid personnel evaluation, health data, customer details and trade secrets in the initial exercise. Define who may see source material, drafts and review comments.
2. Map all processors, subprocessors, locations, transfer mechanisms and deletion/retention behavior, including logs, backups, embeddings and provider-side state; contract terms and actual settings matter. Have Japanese counsel assess APPI obligations, the relevant entrusted-processing or foreign-transfer rules and employee notices for the actual flow. The PPC says businesses should verify personal information input is within the specified purpose and, for personal data in prompts, sufficiently confirm the provider will not use it for machine learning and other relevant conditions. [PPC generative-AI FAQ](https://www.ppc.go.jp/all_fad_index/fad2-3-3-q1/); [PPC notice](https://www.ppc.go.jp/news/careful_information/230602_AI_utilize_alert/).
3. Apply least privilege, encryption in transit and at rest, secret management, upload scanning and limits, security updates, audit logs without raw sensitive prompts, incident response, deletion and restore exercises. Obtain customer approval before any real data leaves its agreed boundary. Use METI/MIC's current AI business guidelines and checklist as governance references, not as an automatic certification of compliance. [METI AI Guidelines for Business v1.2](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/ai_shakai_jisso/20260331_report.html).
4. If evaluating OpenAI as one example, its [API data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) describe training, abuse-monitoring and endpoint-dependent retention controls. Verify current endpoint, account eligibility, contract, region and settings before use; “not used for training” does not mean no retention or that a Japanese residency requirement is met. Other providers require equivalent due diligence. No provider has been selected or authorized in this report.
5. Humans own decisions: expert reviews source fidelity and safety; the knowledge owner publishes and sets expiry; IT/security manages access; sponsor decides continuation. Users can correct and withdraw material through an agreed process. Never promise complete extraction of tacit knowledge or replace supervised practice with a generated instruction.

## Pilot design and success metrics

**Proposed 6–8 week pilot, subject to sponsor agreement:** one site, one non-safety-critical task, one expert, one knowledge owner, one reviewer and 4–8 novices; only synthetic or customer-approved sanitized inputs initially. Week 1: select tasks, agree access and collect baseline. Weeks 2–3: manual capture and expert review. Weeks 4–5: novices perform comparable supervised tasks. Weeks 6–8: repeat, collect feedback, compare cost and decide whether to extend. Optional AI arm only after the privacy, provider and cost gates above are agreed. Define tasks, acceptable errors and thresholds before starting. Small numbers make results directional; report counts and individual ranges, not population-wide claims.

| Measure | Definition and collection | Illustrative decision threshold, not forecast |
|---|---|---|
| Fidelity / safety | Independent expert reviews each claim against the approved source; count unsupported or materially incorrect claims and severe errors; report denominator and reviewer disagreement. | 0 critical unsafe items published; ≥90% of applicable claims supported and correct before approval. Suspend on any critical unsafe output and investigate. |
| Time to approved instruction | Minutes for interview + drafting + expert review + corrections + approval per comparable instruction, including all participants. Compare with current practice on matched tasks. | Median total time no worse than baseline; seek ≥20% reduction, with absolute times and range. |
| Novice task performance | Percentage completing an agreed supervised scenario without critical error; time, escalations and quality assessed with same rubric before/after or against a comparable control. | ≥80% complete without critical error; zero critical errors accepted as success. Note learning and task-difficulty bias. |
| Uptake and freshness | Approved items actually used / eligible items, with voluntary short feedback; items with owner and unexpired review date / approved items. | ≥70% use in relevant opportunities; 100% assigned owner and review date. |
| AI-specific quality and cost, if enabled | Unsupported-claim rate = unsupported substantive claims / reviewed substantive claims; review time; model spend + infrastructure + labor per approved item. Record provider, prompt/version, sources and sample size. | No critical unsafe output published; cost and total time versus manual arm disclosed; stop if safety or budget cap is exceeded. |

Where feasible, assign comparable tasks or participants to manual and AI-assisted workflows, randomize order, blind the reviewer to arm and capture baseline before intervention. If this is infeasible, document selection, order, missing data and training effects and avoid causal claims. A pilot's favorable results do not prove broad rollout, demographic fairness or model reliability.

## Open decisions and research limits

Select a willing sponsor and task; agree the success threshold, baseline, worker participation and scope; decide local-only versus private hosting; confirm data classification, residency, vendor and budget; establish ownership and maintenance. Next, test desirability through discovery and a manual workshop before building AI infrastructure. No market sizing, pricing, legal opinion, live customer evidence or independent validation of the prior paper's claims was attempted. Sources and technical documentation can change; recheck them at implementation and procurement.
